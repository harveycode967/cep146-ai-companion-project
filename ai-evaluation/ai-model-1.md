# 🚨 Regulation fact-check — VERY IMPORTANT

I checked your claims against the **official Chinese regulation**, rather than relying only on English-language news coverage.

The official regulation is:

**《人工智能拟人化互动服务管理暂行办法》**

It was issued April 10, 2026 and became effective July 15, 2026. 

Your overall understanding is **substantially correct**, but there are several places where your presentation should be tightened.

## ✅ Correct

Your claim that it targets AI services involving **simulation of human personality characteristics, thinking patterns and communication styles for continuous emotional interaction** is accurate. That's essentially what Article 2 says. 

Your claim that it is **not simply a ban on ordinary chatbots** is also correct. The scope is specifically anthropomorphic/continuous emotional interaction services. 

Your claim about a **two-hour reminder** is accurate. Article 18 requires reminders when continuous use exceeds two hours. 

Your claim about **minor restrictions** is also substantially accurate. Article 14 prohibits providing virtual relatives/virtual partners to minors and establishes additional protections for users under 14. 

Your claim concerning **crisis intervention** is supported. Article 13 specifically addresses extreme emotional situations and situations involving threats to life/health. 

---

## ⚠️ Claim I'd change: "block virtual relationships for minors entirely"

Your script says:

> "block virtual relationships for minors entirely."

That's **too broad**.

The regulation specifically says providers **may not provide virtual relatives, virtual partners, and other virtual intimate relationship services to minors**. But it also allows certain other anthropomorphic interaction services for minors, subject to additional protections; under-14 users require parental/guardian consent. 

Better:

**"Virtual intimate-relationship services, such as virtual partners and virtual relatives, cannot be provided to minors."**

That's much more legally precise.

---

# ⚠️ Another important correction: data/export

Your discussion question is:

> "Should a company be legally required to let you export your conversation history before shutting down an AI product?"

That's a **great discussion question**, but be careful not to imply the Chinese regulation itself created a general right to export conversations.

In fact, Article 16 says providers must give users options to **copy and delete** interaction data, including historical chat data. 

So your broader criticism about companies deleting/limiting access to histories is a legitimate societal question, but you should distinguish:

**"What the regulation requires"**

from

**"What happened when companies shut down their features."**

That distinction will make your presentation much stronger.

---

# 🚨 One especially important issue

Your research brief says:

> "No training on user data without consent."

That's too simplistic.

The official text says providers generally must protect interaction data and cannot provide it to third parties except under specified circumstances. It specifically says **sensitive personal information contained in interaction data cannot be used for model training without separate consent**, unless otherwise provided by law/administrative regulations. 

So I'd change your wording to:

**"Sensitive personal information contained in users' interaction data generally cannot be used for model training without separate consent, subject to legal exceptions."**

Much more accurate.
