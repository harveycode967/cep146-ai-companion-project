# Video Script: "The Companions That Got Switched Off"

Target runtime: 2:54, inside the 2:30 to 3:00 window.
Word count: about 434 words at roughly 150 words per minute.
Speakers: 2. Speaker A takes the first half, Speaker B takes the second half.

Structure: A covers what happened, B covers what the rule is and why it matters. One handover in the middle rather than four, so each person records one continuous take.

Format: mixed. Speaker A is a live recording on camera. Speaker B is a screen recording of slides.html with voiceover. The guidelines allow any format, so mixing is fine. No AI generated voices, the voice has to be your own.

Slide cues match slides.html. Open it fullscreen, press H to hide the progress bar, advance with the arrow keys.

## SPEAKER A, 0:00 to 1:24, slides 1 to 4, about 209 words

**Slide 1: title**

On July 15th this year, millions of people in China opened an app and found that someone they talked to every day was gone.

Not a person. An AI.

We're group [NAME], and our topic is China's new AI companion regulation, the first rulebook anywhere aimed specifically at AI that acts like a human being.

**Advance to slide 2: the three apps**

Three of China's biggest AI apps, ByteDance's Doubao, Alibaba's Qwen, and Tencent's Yuanbao, switched off their AI companion features rather than try to follow the new rules. We wanted to know why.

**Advance to slide 3: the timeline**

So what did the companies do? They didn't rebuild. They shut it down.

Tencent went first in late June. Alibaba's Qwen disabled user created agents on July 10th, with no migration path. Configurations and chat histories are simply deleted.

ByteDance's Doubao gave people a little more room. Read only access until October 15th, then it's gone. Their advice for saving years of conversations was to take screenshots.

**Advance to slide 4: 345 million**

Doubao is reported as China's most used AI app, with around 345 million monthly users. On Weibo, people described these agents as long term emotional support and complained there was no real way to export any of it.

That's the part that stuck with our group. Your photos have an export button. Your conversations don't.

## SPEAKER B, 1:24 to 2:54, slides 5 to 8, about 225 words

**Slide 5: covered vs not covered**

So what does this rule actually say?

It's called the Interim Measures for Anthropomorphic AI Interaction Services. China's internet regulator issued it in April with four other government agencies, and it took effect on July 15th.

Here's the part that gets misreported. This is not a ban on chatbots. Work assistants, customer service bots, and study tools are specifically excluded. It only covers AI built for ongoing emotional interaction. Companions, virtual partners, emotional support bots.

**Advance to slide 6: the four requirements**

For those, providers have to constantly disclose that the AI isn't human, show anti addiction reminders after long sessions, route users toward help if they show signs of distress, and block virtual relationships for minors entirely.

**Advance to slide 7: why it matters**

Two things make this bigger than one country's policy.

First, all three companies decided that deleting the feature was easier than making it compliant. The things that make an AI companion feel personal, the memory, the persistence, the personality, are the same things a regulator can't easily approve.

Second, this is a template. It arrives in the same year that Character.AI and Google settled lawsuits in the US over chatbots and teen mental health. Every other regulator now has a worked example sitting on the table.

**Advance to slide 8: the discussion question, hold for 5 seconds**

Which leaves us with this. If an AI companion genuinely helped someone through a hard time, is deleting it a safety measure, or a harm in itself? And who should get to decide?

## Before you record

Rewrite your own half in your own words. Read it out loud once. If a sentence feels awkward in your mouth, change it. Delivery that sounds like someone reading off a screen is the most obvious tell there is, and the rubric rewards an engaging video.
